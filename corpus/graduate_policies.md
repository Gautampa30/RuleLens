## Preamble

This policy governs all matters specific to graduate study at Ashford University that are not fully addressed by the Ashford University Academic Regulations (AUR-2024-001). Where this policy and the Academic Regulations address the same matter, the applicable rule is determined by Section 1.1 of the Academic Regulations. Graduate students should read this policy in conjunction with the Academic Regulations and their programme-specific Graduate Programme Handbook.

---

## Section 1: Admission to Graduate Programmes

### 1.1 General Admission Requirements

Admission to a graduate programme at Ashford University requires:

- An undergraduate degree from a recognised institution, normally with a minimum final GPA of 3.0 or equivalent.
- Submission of all required documentation by the published application deadline.
- Two academic references from persons qualified to assess the applicant's readiness for graduate-level study.
- A statement of research intent or professional objectives, as specified by the programme.
- Evidence of English language proficiency where applicable.

Individual programmes may specify additional or more stringent admission requirements through their Graduate Programme Handbooks. The Graduate Studies Committee retains the right to admit applicants on the basis of demonstrated professional or research experience in lieu of any single standard criterion, subject to approval by the Dean of Graduate Studies.

### 1.2 Conditional Admission

An applicant who does not fully satisfy the standard admission criteria may be offered Conditional Admission. A Conditional Admission offer specifies:

- The conditions that must be met before full admission is granted.
- The deadline by which conditions must be met.
- The consequences of failing to satisfy the conditions, which may include withdrawal of the offer of admission.

Conditional Admission students may register for courses and are bound by all academic regulations. They may not be awarded a graduate degree while conditions remain outstanding.

### 1.3 Transfer of Credit

Graduate students may apply to transfer a maximum of twelve credit hours earned at another institution toward their Ashford University graduate programme, subject to the following:

- The credits were earned at a graduate level at a recognised institution.
- The credits were earned within the preceding five years.
- The prior coursework is assessed as equivalent to a required or elective course in the graduate programme.
- A grade of B or higher was achieved in the relevant course.

Transfer credit applications must be submitted to the Graduate Studies Committee within the first year of enrolment.

## Section 2: Academic Progress for Graduate Students

### 2.1 Registration and Full-Time Equivalency

Graduate students are considered full-time if registered for nine or more credit hours per semester. Part-time graduate registration is permitted but may affect eligibility for certain funding arrangements. Graduate students funded by University assistantships must maintain full-time registration unless a specific exception is approved in writing by the Dean of Graduate Studies.

### 2.2 Minimum GPA and Academic Standing

Graduate students must maintain a cumulative GPA of 3.0 or above, consistent with Section 6.1 of the Academic Regulations. A graduate student whose cumulative GPA falls below 3.0 at the end of any semester will be placed on Academic Probation. The student must raise their GPA to 3.0 or above within two consecutive semesters to avoid Academic Dismissal.

### 2.3 Academic Improvement Plan for Graduate Students

A graduate student placed on Academic Probation must meet with their faculty supervisor (where applicable) and their Programme Director within the first two weeks of the following semester to develop an Academic Improvement Plan (AIP). The AIP is reviewed by the Graduate Studies Committee. A student who fails to engage with the AIP process may be referred for Academic Dismissal without further warning.

### 2.4 Course Load During Probation

A graduate student on Academic Probation may not register for more than nine credit hours per semester until they have returned to Good Academic Standing.

### 2.5 Academic Dismissal of Graduate Students

A graduate student who fails to meet the minimum GPA requirement within the allowed probationary period is subject to Academic Dismissal. Unlike undergraduate dismissal (which takes effect following a Dean's review), graduate dismissal requires approval by the Graduate Studies Committee and ratification by the Dean of Graduate Studies. The student shall receive written notice and is entitled to submit a written statement within ten business days before the decision is finalised.

### 2.6 Late Course Withdrawal for Graduate Students

**[C-001 — Source B]**

**Graduate students seeking to withdraw from a course after the end of Week 8 must submit their Late Course Withdrawal request to the Office of Graduate Studies. Such requests must be reviewed and approved by the Graduate Studies Committee, which meets bi-weekly. The Graduate Studies Committee is the sole approving authority for graduate late withdrawals; requests may not be approved by a faculty Dean acting alone.**

Approval decisions of the Graduate Studies Committee are communicated to the student within five business days of the relevant Committee meeting. A student seeking an urgent decision between Committee meetings may request an expedited review by the Chair of the Graduate Studies Committee.

Documentation requirements for graduate late withdrawal are identical to those described in Section 5.3 of the Academic Regulations (serious medical illness, bereavement, or serious unforeseen emergency).

## Section 3: Supervision and Research

### 3.1 Appointment of Supervisors

Graduate students undertaking thesis or research-based programmes must be assigned a primary supervisor within the first semester of enrolment. The supervisor is responsible for:

- Providing academic guidance and feedback on research work.
- Monitoring the student's progress toward completion.
- Certifying that the thesis or research project meets the required scholarly standard.

A student who has not been assigned a supervisor by the end of the first semester should report this to the Programme Director immediately.

### 3.2 Change of Supervisor

A graduate student may request a change of supervisor by submitting a written request to the Programme Director. Requests will be considered by the Graduate Studies Committee. A change of supervisor does not extend the programme duration or adjust any thesis submission deadline without a separate formal application.

### 3.3 Co-Supervision

Some graduate programmes permit or require co-supervision. Where a student has two supervisors, one shall be designated the primary supervisor and one the secondary supervisor. All formal approvals requiring supervisor sign-off require the signature of the primary supervisor. The secondary supervisor may provide input and shall be listed on the thesis.

### 3.4 Supervisory Meetings

Students and supervisors are expected to meet at minimum once per month during the active research period. A record of meetings should be maintained and made available to the Programme Director upon request. Persistent failure to maintain this meeting schedule by either party should be reported to the Programme Director.

## Section 4: Research Integrity

### 4.1 Obligations

Graduate students are expected to conduct research according to the highest ethical and scholarly standards. This includes:

- Obtaining all necessary ethics approvals before commencing data collection.
- Correctly and fully attributing all sources.
- Maintaining accurate and reproducible research records.
- Reporting any suspected breach of research integrity to the Research Integrity Officer.

### 4.2 Ethics Review

Research involving human participants, animals, or sensitive data must be approved by the University Research Ethics Board (UREB) before commencement. Ethics approval must be obtained before any data collection begins, without exception.

Retrospective ethics approval is not available. Data collected without prior ethics approval may not be used in a thesis or publication and may be subject to investigation under the Academic Integrity policy.

### 4.3 Data Retention

Research data supporting a thesis or publication must be retained by the student and the University for a minimum of ten years after the completion or abandonment of the project. Procedures for data storage and transfer upon graduation are set out in the University Research Data Management Policy.

## Section 5: Thesis and Dissertation Requirements

### 5.1 Thesis Format and Submission Requirements

All graduate theses and dissertations must conform to the format requirements specified in the *Ashford University Thesis Formatting Guide*, which is maintained by the Office of Graduate Studies. Deviations from the formatting guide must be approved by the Programme Director before submission.

Theses must be submitted to the Office of Graduate Studies by the published thesis submission deadline for the relevant semester. A thesis received after the deadline shall not be forwarded for examination until the following semester, unless an extension has been granted under Section 5.4.

### 5.2 Thesis Examination

A thesis shall be examined by a panel of at least two examiners, one of whom must be external to the University. The examination typically includes a written component (examiners' reports) and an oral defence. The outcome of the examination is one of:

- Pass with no corrections required
- Pass with minor corrections (to be completed within twelve weeks)
- Pass with major corrections (to be completed within one academic year)
- Resubmission required (a revised thesis to be submitted within one academic year)
- Fail

### 5.3 Oral Defence

The oral defence (viva voce) must be held within three months of the thesis being circulated to the examination panel. The student must be notified of the date at least four weeks in advance. The primary supervisor may attend the oral defence as an observer but may not participate in questioning or deliberations.

### 5.4 Thesis Submission Extension

**[C-003 — Source A]**

**A registered thesis student may apply to the Graduate Studies Committee for an extension to the approved thesis submission deadline. Extensions are granted only in circumstances of documented, exceptional difficulty that could not reasonably have been anticipated or avoided. The maximum extension that may be granted under this provision is one academic term (sixteen weeks).**

Extension applications must be submitted at least four weeks before the current thesis submission deadline and must include:

- A written statement from the student explaining the exceptional circumstances.
- A statement from the primary supervisor assessing the student's progress and the likelihood of completing within the requested extension period.
- Relevant supporting documentation (e.g., medical certificates, evidence of significant technical difficulty affecting the research).

Only one extension may be granted per student per thesis. A student who cannot complete the thesis within the approved extension period must apply for a Leave of Absence or voluntarily withdraw.

### 5.5 Thesis Repository

All approved theses are deposited in the Ashford University Thesis Repository and are made publicly accessible, subject to any embargo period approved by the Graduate Studies Committee. Embargoes may be granted for a maximum of two years where there are documented commercial sensitivity or patent considerations.

## Section 6: Graduation and Credential Award for Graduate Students

### 6.1 Graduation Requirements

In addition to the requirements specified in Chapter 8 of the Academic Regulations, graduate students must:

- Complete all programme-specific requirements including any required colloquia, residencies, or internship components.
- Successfully pass the thesis or dissertation examination (where applicable).
- Clear any outstanding corrections flagged by the examination panel within the specified period.
- Obtain the formal certification of completion from the Programme Director.

### 6.2 Posthumous and In Absentia Awards

A graduate degree may be awarded in absentia where the student is physically unable to attend the Commencement ceremony. The student must notify the Registrar in writing at least three weeks before the ceremony. A degree may be awarded posthumously to a student who has completed all academic requirements prior to death. The posthumous award process is managed by the Dean of Graduate Studies in consultation with the family.
