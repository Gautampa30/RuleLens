## Chapter 1: General Provisions

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

These Academic Regulations govern all students enrolled at Ashford University in undergraduate and graduate programmes. They set out the rights and obligations of students and the University with respect to academic matters, including registration, assessment, progression, withdrawal, and graduation. These regulations apply to all campuses and all modes of study unless an explicit exemption is stated.

Where a conflict exists between these regulations and programme-specific handbooks, these regulations shall take precedence unless the programme-specific document has been explicitly approved as a superseding instrument by the Academic Senate.

### 1.2 Definitions

For the purposes of these regulations:

- **Academic Year** means the period from 1 September to 31 August of the following calendar year, comprising two standard semesters (Autumn and Spring) and one Summer session.
- **Semester** means either the Autumn Semester (early September to late December) or the Spring Semester (early January to late April), each consisting of fifteen teaching weeks plus one examination period of up to three weeks.
- **Summer Session** means the condensed teaching period from May to August.
- **Full-Time Student** means a student registered for twelve or more credit hours per semester.
- **Part-Time Student** means a student registered for fewer than twelve credit hours per semester.
- **Good Academic Standing** means the academic status of a student who satisfies the minimum cumulative GPA requirement specified in Chapter 6 and has no unresolved academic integrity findings.
- **Academic Probation** means the status assigned to a student whose cumulative GPA falls below the minimum threshold, as described in Chapter 6.
- **Programme Director** means the academic staff member responsible for oversight of a named degree programme.
- **Dean** means the senior academic officer of a faculty, responsible for academic governance within that faculty.
- **Registrar** means the administrative officer responsible for maintaining academic records and overseeing registration processes.
- **Credit Hour** means one hour of scheduled instruction per week over one semester, together with a minimum of two hours of independent study per week.

### 1.3 Amendments to These Regulations

These regulations may be amended by the Academic Senate. Amendments take effect from the date specified in the amendment notice. Students who commenced their programme before an amendment takes effect shall be governed by the regulations in force at the time of their commencement unless they choose to opt in to the revised regulations or unless the amendment is required for compliance with external statutory or accreditation requirements.

The Registrar shall maintain a publicly accessible archive of all prior versions of these regulations, each version clearly dated.

### 1.4 Student Responsibility

Students are responsible for familiarising themselves with these regulations and with any additional requirements of their programme. Ignorance of a regulation shall not constitute grounds for exemption from its application. The University will make these regulations readily accessible on the student portal and in printed form at the Registrar's Office.

## Chapter 2: The Academic Calendar

### 2.1 Publication of the Calendar

The Academic Calendar for each year is approved by the Academic Senate and published no later than 31 March of the preceding academic year. The calendar specifies all key dates including:

- Start and end of each semester and the Summer session
- Registration periods and add/drop deadlines
- Examination periods
- Grade submission deadlines
- Commencement ceremonies
- Public and University holidays

### 2.2 Semester Structure

Each standard semester is structured as follows:

| Week | Activity |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Orientation, add/drop period |
| Weeks 3–8 | Teaching period |
| Week 8 (Friday) | Course withdrawal without academic penalty deadline |
| Weeks 9–13 | Teaching period continues |
| Week 14 | Reading week (no scheduled classes) |
| Week 15 | Final teaching week |
| Weeks 16–18 | Examination period |

### 2.3 Summer Session Structure

The Summer session runs for ten weeks of teaching followed by one week of examinations. Credit hours earned during the Summer session carry full academic weight equivalent to credits earned during a standard semester.

## Chapter 3: Registration and Enrolment

### 3.1 Registration Requirement

All students must formally register for each semester and Summer session in which they intend to study. A student who has not completed registration by the end of the add/drop period shall not be permitted to sit examinations or receive credit for any course in that period.

### 3.2 Course Selection and Load Limits

Students may register for a maximum of eighteen credit hours per semester without special permission from the Registrar. A student wishing to take more than eighteen credit hours must obtain approval from their Programme Director and demonstrate a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.5 from the preceding semester.

Part-time students may register for a maximum of nine credit hours per semester.

### 3.3 Add/Drop Period

Students may add or drop courses without academic or financial penalty during the add/drop period, which runs for the first two weeks of each semester. After the close of the add/drop period and up to the end of Week 8, a student may withdraw from a course with a grade of W (Withdrawal) recorded on their transcript. After Week 8, course withdrawal is governed by Section 5.3 (Late Course Withdrawal).

### 3.4 Auditing Courses

A student may audit a course with the permission of the instructor and the Programme Director. An audited course does not earn credit hours and does not appear on the official transcript. Auditors may not sit examinations and are not eligible for a grade.

### 3.5 Repeat Enrolment

A student may repeat a course in which they received a failing grade or a grade of W. When a course is repeated, only the most recent grade is used in the calculation of the cumulative GPA, though both enrolment records appear on the official transcript. A student may not repeat a passed course for the purpose of GPA improvement without approval from the Programme Director.

## Chapter 4: Assessment, Grading, and Academic Records

### 4.1 Grading Scale

The University uses the following grading scale:

| Grade | GPA Points | Percentage Range | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|---|
| A+ | 4.0 | 90–100 | Outstanding |
| A | 4.0 | 85–89 | Excellent |
| A– | 3.7 | 80–84 | Very Good |
| B+ | 3.3 | 77–79 | Good Plus |
| B | 3.0 | 73–76 | Good |
| B– | 2.7 | 70–72 | Good Minus |
| C+ | 2.3 | 67–69 | Satisfactory Plus |
| C | 2.0 | 63–66 | Satisfactory |
| C– | 1.7 | 60–62 | Satisfactory Minus |
| D+ | 1.3 | 57–59 | Passing Plus |
| D | 1.0 | 50–56 | Passing |
| F | 0.0 | 0–49 | Failing |
| W | — | — | Withdrawal |
| I | — | — | Incomplete |
| AU | — | — | Audit |

### 4.2 Grade Submission

Instructors must submit final grades to the Registrar within five business days of the last scheduled examination for the course. For courses with no formal examination, the deadline is five business days after the submission deadline for the final assessment.

### 4.3 Incomplete Grades

An Incomplete (I) grade may be awarded where a student has completed at least 75% of the course requirements and is unable to complete the remaining requirements due to documented circumstances beyond the student's control. The student must request the Incomplete grade from the instructor before the last day of the examination period.

An Incomplete grade must be resolved within eight weeks of the start of the following semester. If the grade is not resolved within this period, the Incomplete shall be converted to a grade of F automatically by the Registrar.

### 4.4 Grade Review and Appeals

A student who believes a final grade has been calculated in error may request a grade review from the instructor within ten business days of the grade being posted on the student portal. If the student remains dissatisfied after the instructor's review, they may submit a formal grade appeal to the Programme Director within a further ten business days. The Programme Director's decision is final unless procedural irregularity is alleged, in which case the matter proceeds under the Academic Appeals Procedure described in the *Ashford University Appeals and Academic Conduct Policy*.

## Chapter 5: Withdrawal and Leave of Absence

### 5.1 Voluntary Withdrawal from the University

A student wishing to withdraw from the University must notify the Registrar in writing. Withdrawal does not require the approval of a faculty member, but the Registrar will refer the student to the Student Advisory Service before processing the withdrawal. Grades of W shall be assigned for all courses in which the student is currently enrolled. Financial obligations arising from enrolment remain subject to the fee refund policy set out in the *Fee Schedule and Financial Obligations Policy*.

### 5.2 Withdrawal from Individual Courses

Withdrawal from individual courses is subject to the timeline specified in Chapter 3. A grade of W is recorded and does not affect GPA. However, a student who accumulates more than four W grades in any single degree programme shall be subject to an academic progress review by the Programme Director.

### 5.3 Late Course Withdrawal

After the course withdrawal deadline at the end of Week 8, a student wishing to withdraw from a course must submit a Late Course Withdrawal request. Such requests may be granted only on the basis of documented exceptional circumstances, which must include at least one of the following:

- Serious medical illness or injury (requiring supporting documentation from a registered medical practitioner)
- Bereavement of an immediate family member
- Serious unforeseen personal or family emergency

**Late withdrawal requests must be reviewed and approved by the Dean of the student's faculty. The Dean may consult with the Programme Director but holds sole authority to approve or deny the request.** Upon approval, a grade of W shall be recorded. Upon denial, the student remains enrolled and shall receive the grade earned.

Requests for late withdrawal must be submitted to the faculty office no later than the last day of the examination period for the semester in question.

### 5.4 Leave of Absence

A student may apply for a Leave of Absence (LOA) for up to two consecutive semesters. Applications must be submitted to the Programme Director with supporting documentation at least four weeks before the start of the semester for which the leave is requested. A student on LOA retains their place in the programme but may not enrol in any courses during the leave period.

A student who does not return from LOA or request an extension by the deadline specified in the LOA approval letter shall be administratively withdrawn from the University.

### 5.5 Medical Withdrawal

A student who is unable to continue studies due to medical reasons may be granted a Medical Withdrawal at any point during a semester. Medical Withdrawals require supporting documentation from a licensed medical or psychiatric practitioner and must be approved by the Dean in consultation with the Student Health Services Director. Upon approval, grades of MW (Medical Withdrawal) shall be recorded for all courses in the current semester. MW grades carry no academic penalty and are treated as W grades for progression purposes.

## Chapter 6: Academic Standing and Progression

### 6.1 Minimum GPA Requirement for Good Academic Standing

**Undergraduate students must maintain a cumulative GPA of 2.0 or above to remain in Good Academic Standing.** Graduate students must maintain a cumulative GPA of 3.0 or above. Failure to meet the minimum GPA requirement at the end of any semester will result in the student being placed on Academic Probation as described in Section 6.2.

### 6.2 Academic Probation

A student is placed on Academic Probation when their cumulative GPA falls below the programme minimum. The student will receive written notice from the Registrar within ten business days of final grades being posted for the semester.

During Academic Probation:
- The student must meet with their academic advisor within the first three weeks of the following semester.
- The student may not register for more than thirteen credit hours per semester.
- The student must complete a personalised Academic Improvement Plan (AIP) approved by the Programme Director.

### 6.3 Continuation on Probation

A student on Academic Probation who achieves the required minimum GPA in the immediately following term will be returned to Good Academic Standing. A student on Academic Probation who does not achieve the required minimum GPA within two consecutive semesters shall be subject to Academic Dismissal as described in Section 6.4.

### 6.4 Academic Dismissal

Academic Dismissal is the permanent separation of a student from their degree programme. A student facing Academic Dismissal will receive a written notice from the Dean and will be afforded the opportunity to submit a written response within ten business days before the dismissal is made final. The dismissed student retains the right to appeal under the Academic Appeals Procedure.

A student who has been academically dismissed may apply for readmission after a minimum period of two years. Readmission is not guaranteed and is subject to the admissions procedures current at the time of reapplication.

### 6.5 Dean's List

A full-time undergraduate student who achieves a semester GPA of 3.7 or higher and has completed at least twelve credit hours in that semester, with no I (Incomplete) grades outstanding, shall be placed on the Dean's List for that semester. The Dean's List is published on the student portal and a letter of recognition is sent to the student.

## Chapter 7: Financial Obligations

### 7.1 Tuition and Fees

Tuition rates, mandatory fees, and the schedule of deadlines for payment are published each year in the *Fee Schedule and Financial Obligations Policy*. Students are responsible for payment of all applicable tuition and fees by the published deadlines. Failure to pay tuition by the deadline may result in administrative deregistration from courses.

### 7.2 Refund Policy for Withdrawal

The tuition refund schedule applicable upon voluntary withdrawal or course withdrawal is as follows:

| Timing of Withdrawal | Refund Percentage |
|---|---|
| During add/drop period (Weeks 1–2) | 100% |
| Weeks 3–4 | 75% |
| Weeks 5–6 | 50% |
| Week 7 | 25% |
| Week 8 and beyond | 0% |

No tuition refund is applicable for medical withdrawals processed after the end of Week 6. Medical Withdrawal fee waivers may be considered on a case-by-case basis by the Bursar's Office with supporting documentation.

### 7.3 Late Payment Fee

A late payment fee of $75 shall be applied to any tuition balance outstanding after the published payment deadline. A further late payment fee of $75 shall be applied for each additional thirty-day period that the balance remains unpaid, up to a maximum of three such charges per semester.

### 7.4 Financial Holds

The University may place a financial hold on a student's account where tuition, fees, or library fines are outstanding. A student with a financial hold may not register for future semesters, receive official transcripts, or participate in Commencement ceremonies until all obligations are resolved.

## Chapter 8: Graduation

### 8.1 Eligibility for Graduation

To be eligible for graduation, a student must:

1. Complete all courses required by their programme's curriculum with a passing grade.
2. Satisfy the minimum credit hour requirement of their programme.
3. Achieve a cumulative GPA of at least 2.0 (undergraduate) or 3.0 (graduate).
4. Resolve all Incomplete (I) grades.
5. Have no outstanding financial holds.
6. Submit an Application for Graduation by the published deadline.

### 8.2 Application for Graduation

Students must submit an Application for Graduation to the Registrar by the following deadlines:

- For May Commencement: 1 February of the graduation year
- For December Commencement: 1 September of the graduation year

Late applications may be considered on payment of a Late Graduation Application Fee as listed in the *Fee Schedule and Financial Obligations Policy*.

### 8.3 Graduation Audit

Upon receipt of an Application for Graduation, the Registrar shall conduct a graduation audit within twenty business days to verify that all programme requirements have been or are on track to be met. The Registrar will notify the student of any deficiencies. Students who fail to clear all deficiencies before the Commencement date will participate in Commencement provisionally but will not receive their degree until all requirements are met.

### 8.4 Honours Classifications

Undergraduate degrees may be awarded with one of the following honours classifications based on cumulative GPA:

| Classification | Cumulative GPA |
|---|---|
| Summa Cum Laude | 3.90 – 4.00 |
| Magna Cum Laude | 3.70 – 3.89 |
| Cum Laude | 3.50 – 3.69 |

Graduate degrees are not subject to honours classification under these regulations but may carry distinctions as specified by individual programme regulations.

## Chapter 9: Student Records and Privacy

### 9.1 Official Transcripts

Official transcripts are issued only by the Registrar's Office and bear the official University seal and the signature of the Registrar. Requests for official transcripts must be submitted in writing (or through the designated student portal process) with a minimum of five business days' notice. A transcript fee applies as listed in the *Fee Schedule and Financial Obligations Policy*.

Students are entitled to one complimentary set of transcripts upon graduation. Additional transcripts are subject to the standard transcript fee.

### 9.2 Student Record Access

Students have the right to inspect their own academic records. Requests to inspect records must be submitted in writing to the Registrar. The Registrar must provide access or a copy within fifteen business days of the request. Records may not be released to third parties without the student's written consent, except where required by law or court order.

### 9.3 Amendment of Records

A student who believes their academic record contains a factual error (as distinct from a grade disagreement, which is governed by Section 4.4) may request correction by submitting evidence to the Registrar. The Registrar shall investigate and respond within twenty business days.

## Chapter 10: Disability and Accessibility Accommodations

### 10.1 Entitlement to Reasonable Accommodations

Students with documented disabilities are entitled to reasonable accommodations to support their full participation in academic life. Accommodations do not alter the fundamental nature or academic standards of any programme.

### 10.2 Registration with Disability Services

Students seeking accommodations must register with the Office of Disability Services (ODS) and provide documentation of the disability from a qualified professional. ODS will work with the student and their instructors to establish an Accommodation Plan.

### 10.3 Examination Accommodations

Examination accommodations may include extended time, a separate examination room, the use of assistive technology, or other adjustments as determined appropriate by ODS. Accommodation Plans must be communicated to instructors at least two weeks before the first scheduled examination for which accommodations are required.

### 10.4 Appeals Regarding Accommodations

A student who believes they have been denied appropriate accommodations may file a complaint with the Office of the Vice-Chancellor (Academic Affairs) within twenty business days of the denial. The complaint procedure is set out in the *Ashford University Equity and Inclusion Policy*.
