## Part 1: Academic Appeals

### 1.1 Scope of This Policy

This policy establishes the procedures by which students may formally appeal academic decisions, including grade appeals, programme dismissal decisions, examination results, and other matters governed by the Academic Regulations. A student who believes that an academic decision was made in error, that proper procedures were not followed, or that exceptional circumstances were not given adequate weight, may appeal under this policy.

This policy does not provide a general mechanism for re-marking or re-examining submitted work. It provides a process for reviewing whether proper procedure was followed and whether relevant evidence was fairly considered.

### 1.2 Grounds for Appeal

Valid grounds for an academic appeal include:

1. **Procedural irregularity**: A procedure required by university policy was not followed and this irregularity had a material effect on the outcome.
2. **New evidence**: Evidence exists that was not available at the time of the original decision and which, had it been available, may have materially changed the outcome.
3. **Inadequate consideration of exceptional circumstances**: The student submitted documented exceptional circumstances that were not given adequate weight in the original decision.

Disagreement with the academic judgement of an examiner or assessor is not by itself a valid ground for appeal unless accompanied by evidence of procedural irregularity, bias, or demonstrable factual error.

### 1.3 Appeal Timeline

| Stage | Action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 (Informal) | Student contacts instructor or Programme Director | Within 10 business days of decision |
| Stage 2 (Formal Faculty) | Written appeal to Dean of Faculty | Within 10 business days of Stage 1 outcome |
| Stage 3 (University) | Written appeal to Academic Appeals Panel | Within 15 business days of Stage 2 outcome |
| Stage 4 (Review) | Request for procedural review by Ombudsperson | Within 10 business days of Stage 3 outcome |

Appeals submitted outside the applicable deadline will not normally be considered unless the student can demonstrate that circumstances beyond their control prevented timely submission.

### 1.4 Academic Appeals Panel

The Academic Appeals Panel is a standing body convened by the Vice-Chancellor (Academic Affairs). Its composition for each hearing is:

- One senior academic staff member nominated by the Academic Senate (Chair)
- One academic staff member from a faculty not involved in the original decision
- One student representative from the Student Union

Panel members who have a conflict of interest with the appealing student or the matter under appeal must recuse themselves. Panel decisions are reached by majority vote and are communicated to the student in writing within fifteen business days of the hearing.

### 1.5 Outcomes of an Appeal

The Academic Appeals Panel may:

- Uphold the original decision
- Overturn the original decision
- Remit the matter to the original decision-maker for reconsideration with specified guidance
- Order a fresh assessment or examination

The Panel may not directly award a grade or substitute its academic judgement for that of the original assessor. If the Panel overturns a decision, the matter is referred to the appropriate academic authority for determination.

## Part 2: Academic Integrity

### 2.1 Standards of Academic Integrity

Ashford University upholds the principle that all assessed work submitted by students must be the student's own work, produced in accordance with the rules of the course and the assessment task. Violations of academic integrity undermine the value of awards and are treated seriously.

### 2.2 Definitions of Academic Misconduct

The following constitute academic misconduct:

- **Plagiarism**: Presenting another person's words, ideas, or work as one's own without proper attribution.
- **Fabrication**: Inventing or falsifying data, sources, results, or other information in assessed work.
- **Collusion**: Working with another student on an assessment intended to be completed individually, where such collaboration has not been authorised.
- **Contract Cheating**: Paying, arranging, or requesting another person to complete assessed work on one's behalf.
- **Exam Misconduct**: Possessing or attempting to use unauthorised materials or devices during an examination; communicating with another student during an examination without authorisation.
- **Self-Plagiarism**: Submitting work that has been previously assessed in another course, at Ashford University or elsewhere, without proper disclosure and approval.

### 2.3 Investigation Procedure

All suspected academic misconduct must be reported to the Academic Integrity Officer (AIO) by the instructor or any other member of the University community who becomes aware of it. The AIO will conduct a preliminary review within ten business days to determine whether the matter warrants a formal investigation.

If a formal investigation is opened, the student will be notified in writing and provided:
- A description of the alleged misconduct
- Copies of any evidence gathered
- An invitation to submit a written response within fifteen business days

A formal hearing may be convened at the discretion of the AIO.

### 2.4 Possible Sanctions for Academic Misconduct

Where academic misconduct is confirmed, sanctions may include, but are not limited to:

| Sanction | Typical Application |
|---|---|
| Written warning | Minor or first-time infringement |
| Grade reduction for the assessment | Moderate plagiarism or unauthorised collaboration |
| Grade of zero for the assessment | Serious or deliberate misconduct |
| Grade of F for the course | Persistent or aggravated misconduct |
| Notation on academic transcript | Confirmed contract cheating or fabrication |
| Suspension from the University | Egregious or repeated misconduct |
| Expulsion from the University | Extreme or repeated egregious misconduct |

Sanctions are applied proportionately to the severity and circumstances of the misconduct. A student's disciplinary history at the University will be taken into account.

## Part 3: Academic Probation, Dismissal, and Reinstatement

### 3.1 Probationary Requirements for Undergraduate Students

Undergraduate students placed on Academic Probation under Section 6.2 of the Academic Regulations must meet the following conditions:

- Register for no more than thirteen credit hours in the probationary semester.
- Meet with their academic advisor at least twice during the probationary semester.
- Complete and adhere to an Academic Improvement Plan approved by the Programme Director.

### 3.2 Minimum GPA to Exit Probation — Undergraduate Students

**[C-002 — Source B]**

**An undergraduate student on Academic Probation must achieve a term GPA of at least 2.3 in the probationary semester to be removed from probationary status. A term GPA of 2.3 or higher in the probationary semester will result in the student being returned to Good Academic Standing, provided their cumulative GPA has also risen to 2.0 or above.**

If the student achieves a term GPA between 2.0 and 2.29, they remain on probation for one additional semester. If the student does not achieve a cumulative GPA of 2.0 or above within two consecutive probationary semesters, they shall be subject to Academic Dismissal.

### 3.3 Minimum GPA to Exit Probation — Graduate Students

A graduate student on Academic Probation must achieve a term GPA of at least 3.0 in the probationary semester. If the student does not raise their cumulative GPA to 3.0 or above within two consecutive probationary semesters, they shall be subject to Academic Dismissal under the procedures in Section 2.5 of the Graduate Studies Policy.

### 3.4 Academic Dismissal Procedure

Upon determination that a student meets the criteria for Academic Dismissal:

1. The Dean (or Graduate Studies Committee for graduate students) issues a formal Notice of Intent to Dismiss.
2. The student may submit a written statement within ten business days.
3. The Dean (or Committee) reviews the statement and makes a final determination.
4. The student is notified of the outcome in writing.

A dismissed student who believes the dismissal procedure was not followed correctly may appeal under Part 1 of this policy.

### 3.5 Reinstatement After Dismissal

An undergraduate student who has been academically dismissed may apply for reinstatement after two years from the date of dismissal. Reinstatement is not automatic and is considered on the merits of the application, including evidence of changed circumstances and academic readiness.

A reinstated student is subject to the academic regulations and programme requirements in force at the time of reinstatement, not those in force at the time of original admission.

## Part 4: Student Conduct Outside Academic Matters

### 4.1 Scope

This Part applies to student conduct that falls outside the academic integrity framework but that may affect the University community, including conduct at University events, on University premises, and online activity that is directed at or significantly impacts University community members.

### 4.2 Student Conduct Officer

The Office of Academic Integrity and Student Conduct appoints a Student Conduct Officer (SCO) who is responsible for investigating and resolving non-academic conduct matters. The SCO works in coordination with Campus Security, the Student Advisory Service, and, where relevant, external authorities.

### 4.3 Sanctions for Non-Academic Conduct

Sanctions may include a formal warning, restrictions on access to University facilities, community service requirements, mandatory participation in educational programmes, suspension, or expulsion. Conduct resulting in the commission of a criminal offence will be reported to the relevant authorities.
